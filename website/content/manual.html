Getting started

  1. Open GenErik DJ.
  2. Allow music-library access when you want to use local songs and playlists.
  3. Open the library with the music button.
  4. Select an available track.
  5. Load it onto a stopped deck.
  6. Check the audio output before you start playback.

Start with two decks. Experimental expansions are not required for the base workspace.

Your workspace

iPad

The browser and decks share the screen. Drag the pane handles to change how much space each part uses.

iPhone

The front-screen browser has Performance, Browse and Expand shortcuts. Use them to change the space available for browsing and deck controls.

iPhone Duo

The front screen uses the phone browser. The inside screen uses the wider workspace, with movable browser and deck panes.

Mac

Use the pointer to select tracks and operate deck controls. Drag Load towards the destination deck. The Mac app reads local songs and playlists from your Music library.

The Mac workspace requires a window of at least 800 × 600 pixels. A dedicated DJ keyboard-shortcut set has not been verified.

Choose your controls

Choose each control separately. You do not need to switch the whole app into a simple or advanced mode.

  1. Open the layout settings.
  2. Find Show on the decks.
  3. Enable the controls you use.
  4. Disable controls you want to hide.

Beat Jump, Tempo fader, Cue button and Headphone cue button each have their own switch. SuperSlicer also has a visibility switch. These choices remain saved between sessions.

Two or four decks

Two decks are the default. Eligible iPad trial builds offer a saved four-deck option, with A/C and B/D selection. Four decks are not currently enabled in the iPhone or Mac source.

Tempo and pitch bend

Enable Tempo fader to put a tempo control on the deck. Pitch bend is a separate controller action: hold the mapped up or down control to adjust playback temporarily. The current layout settings do not contain a dedicated pitch-bend visibility switch.

Make room

Drag the browser and deck dividers to resize the panes. Open EQ, effects and cues when needed, then close them to give the waveform more space.

Touch gestures

  • Slide Load towards the destination deck.
  • Drag the overview to inspect another passage. Pinch it to change the scale.
  • Tap a beat in the main waveform to schedule a jump.
  • Place two fingers on the waveform to create a loop.
  • Move both fingers to move the loop. Pinch or spread them to resize it.
  • Swipe down with both fingers and release to leave the loop, or select LOOP.
  • On the FX pad, move horizontally to change the parameter and vertically to change the mix.

These gestures describe the touch interface. On Mac, use the pointer for the available on-screen controls.

Music library

The library shows songs and playlists from the device’s music library. Use search to narrow the list. Audio must be on the device and accessible to the app.

Protected tracks and unavailable cloud-only audio cannot be loaded. Access to library metadata does not grant access to subscription audio. The app does not change your Music library.

Loading tracks

  1. Select a track in the browser.
  2. Drag Load towards A or B.
  3. Check the track name on the destination deck.

Follow the A and B labels beside Load. In the standard stacked layout, drag up for A and down for B. The file start appears at the playhead when the track loads.

Track analysis

Preparation can include a waveform, BPM, key, beat grid and track sections. Estimated values are not guarantees. Review uncertain timing before a performance.

  1. Load the track and inspect the grid against audible beats.
  2. Open the track’s grid controls when a correction is needed.
  3. Tap the intended beat to mark beat one.
  4. Select Done to save the downbeat and phase correction.

Manual preparation takes priority over automatic sharing. Deep analysis and recovery belong between sets.

Waveforms

The large waveform shows local detail. The overview shows the track’s wider structure and current position. Beat lines, section labels and cue markers provide timing references.

Use the waveform to move through the track. Resize the workspace panes to give the browser or a deck more room. Frequency bands help separate musical detail. They are not isolated audio stems.

Deck controls

A and B each have Play, Sync and contextual tools. Open the deck’s EQ or FX drawer when you need it. Close the drawer to return space to the waveform.

The performance surface supports direct waveform control, beat-based movement, loops and cues. Optional buttons are configured in Settings.

Sync & tempo

The beat grid governs timing. Sync remains enabled until you disable it. With a usable grid, playback starts, seeks and gesture releases preserve or restore alignment.

Temporary manual control does not change the saved Sync choice. A track without a usable grid remains unresolved. Check its timing before a synced transition.

Use the shared tempo control to adjust the mix. Keylock starts enabled for A and B on load unless you change the saved setting.

Cueing

Open the deck’s cue controls to use saved cue positions. Headphone cue is a separate listening route. Set its blend and level in the sound controls.

Separate master and headphone monitoring requires a suitable output arrangement. Hardware routing varies. Check it before the set.

Loops

Use the deck’s touch-loop controls to repeat a passage. Adjust loop size against the beat grid. Check the active loop indication before releasing it.

Freeze, Flux and contextual slicing are present in the development workspace. Their live behaviour still requires device acceptance.

EQ & mixer

Open EQ beside the deck you want to adjust. Use the crossfader to change the balance between A and B. Check output level and headphone routing in Sound & Headphones.

Keep enough output headroom when combining tracks. A silent deck can result from its output route or mixer position even when its waveform moves.

Effects

Open FX beside the deck. Select an effect from its menu and use the shared XY control to change it. Effects start bypassed.

Close the drawer when you finish. The development build includes filtering, delay and reverb. Release availability remains under test.

Recommendations

The browser compares candidate tracks with the selected track. It excludes the selected track and tracks already loaded.

  • Tempo candidates fall within ±6%, including half-time and double-time.
  • Compatible musical keys rank ahead of other tempo matches.
  • Compatible keys share an Open Key number, use adjacent numbers in the same mode, or form a relative-key pair.
  • When the selected track has no BPM, recommendations use available key matches.

These are preparation rules, not a promise that a transition will work. The current browser rules do not rank genre, energy or listening behaviour.

Recording & history

  1. Open the sound panel.
  2. Select Record.
  3. Add a venue or location if needed.
  4. Select Record in the prompt.
  5. Select Stop recording when the set ends.
  6. Wait for the recording to finish saving.

Find recordings and play history in the browser. Use the selected recording’s sharing controls to export it. A location label forms part of the recording filename.

The development purchase policy includes recording limits. Final purchase availability and pricing remain unannounced.

Sharing & recovery

Analysis and sections share through your private iCloud storage while the app is open and decks are idle. This can happen automatically. Audio files are not included.

Under Sharing & backup, Sync now also saves preparation for recovery. Stop sync cancels the current transfer. It is not a permanent opt-out.

  1. Stop playback before restoring preparation.
  2. Open Saved versions.
  3. Find and select the required track version.
  4. Confirm Restore at next launch.
  5. Close and reopen the app.

The selected version replaces that track’s grids, cues and analysis. The previous preparation remains as another saved version.

See privacy choices before removing local or cloud data.

Settings

Settings separates playback preferences, sound and headphones, controllers, library preparation, sharing and recovery. Keep setup and long analysis tasks outside a live performance.

Optional controls change the workspace. Enable only the controls you need.

Controllers

Controller adapters and mapping tools are in development. A mapping in the app is not a verified compatibility claim. No public supported-controller list has been approved.

Contact support with the exact model, firmware and connection method before relying on a controller.

Accessibility

Read the accessibility statement for the distinction between website support and app testing. Contact support if a control prevents you from completing a task.

For other problems, see troubleshooting.
